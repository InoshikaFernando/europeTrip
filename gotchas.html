Gotchas & Things to Watch Out For The stuff that quietly trips families up — Europe & China, 2026

🚕 #1 — Car seats, taxis & the Nordic exemption

The nuance: In Sweden and Denmark, kids under 135 cm must use a seat/booster in a normal car — but taxis are partially exempt. A taxi may legally carry a child without a seat for occasional short trips (under-3s in the back seat; over-3s belted in the back). So a taxi turning up with no seat isn't breaking the law — but it's still the least safe option. All three kids (Avin 4 · 106 cm, Aviann 8 · 122 cm, Avisha 11 · 129 cm) are under 135 cm, so for the rental car they always need the 1 seat + 2 boosters you're bringing. For a Gothenburg/Copenhagen taxi, either book a family taxi with seats ahead, or keep the rides short.

🚗 Driving the rental across borders

One car, eight countries — most of the expensive mistakes live here.

🇮🇹 Italy ZTL zones will fine you by post costly

Historic centres (Florence, Milan, Rome, Naples, Venice-mainland) have camera-enforced ZTL (Limited Traffic Zones). Drive in without a permit and a €80–100+ fine arrives at the rental company months later — per zone, per crossing. Park outside the centre and walk/transit in.

Your hotel can sometimes register your plate — ask before you drive to the door.

🎫 Vignettes must be active before you cross the border buy ahead

Each has its own toll sticker — buy the e-vignette the day before you cross:

  • 🇦🇹 Austria — €11.50 / 10-day "Pickerl" · asfinag.at
  • 🇨🇿 Czechia — €13 / 10-day, fully electronic · edalnice.cz
  • 🇭🇺 Hungary — e-matrica by plate · nemzetiutdij.hu
  • 🇸🇮 Slovenia — €16 / 7-day, needed even for the drive-through · evinjeta.dars.si
  • 🇨🇭 Switzerland — CHF 40, whole-year only (no short option) · via.admin.ch

Germany, Italy & France have no car-sticker vignette (France uses toll booths / autoroute; Italy uses toll booths).

🇫🇷 France needs a Crit'Air sticker for city zones order early

Paris, Lyon and other low-emission zones require a physical Crit'Air windscreen sticker. Order it from the official site only (certificat-air.gouv.fr) — it's ~€3 but ships by post, so allow weeks. Third-party sites overcharge. Remember the cousin's car needs one too if you drive it in.

🅿️ Driving on the right & low-emission zones everywhere habit

Coming from NZ, everything is mirror-image — roundabouts, overtaking, the gear hand. The dangerous moments are pulling out of a car park and the first roundabout each morning. Also: German cities have Umweltzone (green badge) zones and most old-town cores ban or restrict cars — check before routing to a hotel door.

⛽ Fuel, tolls & parking take contactless — but keep a plan money

Many autoroute toll booths and automated petrol pumps in France/Italy are card-only and some reject foreign cards at night. Keep a backup card and some coins. Blue-lined street parking = paid; white = free/resident; yellow = don't.

💳 Money & payments

🇨🇳 China is cashless — but Visa/Mastercard barely work set up first

Almost everything (DiDi, food, Great Wall booths, shops) runs on WeChat Pay / Alipay. Link an international card inside the app before you fly — it's painful to set up on arrival. Carry ~¥2,000 cash as backup; some vendors and taxis still take it, and cards often don't.

At Mutianyu, arrange the DiDi to wait and return — return cars are scarce at the Wall.

🌐 The Great Firewall blocks Google, WhatsApp, Gmail & maps install now

In China you cannot reach Google Maps, Gmail, WhatsApp, Instagram, or most Western apps without a VPN installed before arrival (you can't download one there). Download offline maps and save addresses/tickets locally. WeChat works fine for messaging inside China.

💶 Not all of "Europe" is the euro cash mix

Different currencies on this route: 🇨🇿 Czech koruna, 🇭🇺 Hungarian forint, 🇨🇭 Swiss franc, 🇩🇰 Danish krone, 🇸🇪 Swedish krona. Don't over-withdraw koruna/forint — you can't spend leftovers next door. When a card machine offers to charge in NZD, always choose the local currency (decline "conversion" = better rate).

🧾 Tipping is modest, and tap water is fine relax

No US-style 20%. Round up or ~5–10% for good service; often already included as coperto/servizio in Italy. Tap water is safe across Western Europe & the Nordics — but Italian restaurants will bring (and charge for) bottled unless you ask for "acqua del rubinetto." Public fountains in Rome are drinkable.

🎟️ Museums, churches & timed tickets

🛂 "Free kids" still need a ticket — and their passport carry ID

Under-18s are free at many sights (Uffizi, Louvre, Versailles, Vatican, Doge's Palace), but you must still book a free timed ticket each and carry the kids' passports as age proof. Reduced/youth rates are checked at the door. Many tickets are nominative (name-matched) — book in the traveller's name.

📅 The big museums each close a different day plan around

  • Louvre — closed Tuesdays (open late Wed to 21:45)
  • Versailles — closed Mondays; Passport ticket is the only entry in 2026
  • Uffizi / most Italian state museums — closed Mondays
  • Vatican Museums — closed Sundays (except last Sunday of month)

Book the timed slot weeks ahead — the popular ones sell out 3–4 weeks out in July.

⛪ Church dress code: shoulders & knees covered pack a layer

St Peter's, the Vatican, Milan Duomo, St Mark's and most working churches refuse entry to bare shoulders or above-the-knee shorts — in peak summer heat this catches everyone. Carry a light scarf/shawl. Expect airport-style security queues even with a timed ticket (Sainte-Chapelle, the Vatican). At the Milan Duomo there's no bag storage — leave bags in the car.

🚆 Trains & the Sweden leg

🇸🇪 Copenhagen → Gothenburg: the fast train can sell out have a plan B

The fast X2000 has reserved seats and sells out in summer. If it's full, the Øresundståg regional (CPH → Gothenburg, ~3h40m) is walk-up, no reservation — buy first thing that morning. Border ID checks between Denmark and Sweden happen on some crossings — have passports out.

🇫🇷 Eurostar check-in closes early & old QR codes go void check in on time

Eurostar (Paris → London) has airport-style check-in that shuts ~30–45 min before departure — arrive 75–90 min early with passports; it's an international border. If any flight/train was rebooked, the old boarding-pass QR codes are void — re-check-in online and use only the new ones. Screenshot everything for offline.

🇳🇱🇸🇪 Amsterdam & Sweden: print boarding passes at the kiosk self-service

At Amsterdam Schiphol (AMS) and Swedish airports (Gothenburg/Stockholm/Arlanda), you don't need a phone pass or a paid airline desk — the self-service check-in machines print boarding passes and bag tags for free. Scan your passport or booking code, tag your own bags, and drop them at the automated bag-drop. Handy if the app pass won't load or you want a paper backup — but still check in online first so you're not stuck if the kiosks queue.

🧳 General & the small stuff

🔌 Three different plugs on one trip adapters

Most of Europe is Type C/F (two round pins) — but Switzerland is Type J and Italy sometimes Type L, and both can reject a fat Schuko/universal plug. A slim multi-adapter + a small power strip lets you charge the whole family off one socket. China is Type A/I. All chargers are dual-voltage; the hair tools may not be.

🏪 Sundays & August: shops and pharmacies shut stock up Sat

In Germany, Austria, Switzerland and the Nordics, most shops close Sundays and many pharmacies too. In Italy/France, August is holiday season — some family restaurants and shops close for weeks. Buy nappies/snacks/meds Saturday, and know the nearest on-duty pharmacy.

☀️ July heat + long sightseeing days with a 4-year-old pace it

Rome/Pompeii/Naples hit the mid-30s°C. Pompeii has almost no shade — hats, sunscreen, 2L water each, and a midday break. Book the biggest walks for morning. Carry rehydration sachets and the kids' regular meds (with a copy of scripts).

📵 Offline everything & a roaming plan before you go

Save every ticket PDF, hotel address and map offline — you'll lose signal in Alpine passes, on ferries and (deliberately) in China. An eSIM per adult phone is cheaper than roaming; buy the China one separately or rely on the VPN + WeChat combo.

🛂 Passports: the single most-used document don't pack deep

You'll show them constantly — nominative sight tickets, kid age-proof, hotel check-ins, the Denmark↔Sweden border, real-name ticketing in China, and every flight. Keep them accessible, not in the checked bag, and carry a photo/scan backup of every page in the cloud and on paper.
