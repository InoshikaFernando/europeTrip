No. 03 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
the City of a
Hundred Spires.

Prague survived. That's the line we kept coming back to. It survived the Hussite wars, the Thirty Years' War, Nazi occupation, communist rule, and most miraculously, the wave of Allied bombing that flattened so much of Central Europe in 1945. Walking its cobblestones is walking through the only major European capital that still looks the way it did in the 1700s.

For two summer days we lived inside that surviving city: in a Gold Art Apartment overlooking the Old Town, near the river that splits the city in two. Our days began with cobblestone walks before breakfast, when the Charles Bridge was empty and the morning mist made the statues look like ghosts.

This issue is about a city of red roofs, river barges, and 800 years of Gothic spires — and the kids who learned to say "prosím" and "děkuji" by the end of day one.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

Walk the bridge
before the bridge
walks you.
The Charles Bridge has stood since 1357. By 9am it carries thousands of visitors. We crossed it at 5:30 — empty, misty, alone with thirty baroque saints carved in sandstone.
Tue 14 Jul 2026Karlův most, Old Town to Malá Strana40 min walk · empty crowds at 5:30

HERO PHOTO · TBD
Charles Bridge at first light — 30 baroque statues, 1357 foundation stones, and not a single other person.

You have to do Charles Bridge at sunrise. By eight in the morning it is jammed. By ten you can't move. But at five-thirty in July, when the sky is already light and the city is still half-asleep, it is the most magical 515-metre walk in Europe. The mist sits on the Vltava. The thirty baroque statues of saints — added one by one between 1683 and 1714 — emerge from the gloom like patient guardians.

The city of a hundred spires. — A nickname Prague earned in the 19th century, for its Gothic skyline.

We set our alarms for 4:45. The hotel's night-clerk smiled when we asked her if anyone would be open — "the bridge is always open," she said. We walked down through Old Town Square in absolute silence, past the Astronomical Clock that has shown the hours since 1410, and crossed the bridge as the first sun caught the top of the Lesser Town tower. Avin held my hand the whole way. He whispered, of all things, the word "magic."

OLD TOWN SQUARE · DAY 05
A clock that shows
the cosmos.
Prague's Astronomical Clock has marked time since 1410. Every hour, the twelve apostles parade across the dial, Death rings his bell, and the rooster crows. The kids watched four times.
Mon 13 Jul 2026Staroměstské náměstí2 hrs · ice cream + clock-watching
📷 images/czech/day-05-astronomical-clock.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Astronomical Clock at noon. The apostles march. Death tolls his bell. Then a rooster — added in 1948 — crows.

The Old Town Square is what every other European city centre wishes it were. It has been the heart of Prague since the 12th century. On its perimeter you find the Týn Church with its twin Gothic towers, the rococo Kinský Palace, the old Town Hall, and the city's most famous instrument — the Astronomical Clock. Beneath the clock's blue-and-gold dial, two more dials show the position of the sun in the zodiac and the days of the year. It is a clock and a calendar and a horoscope and an astronomical model, all in one piece of medieval machinery, all still working.

Twenty-four hours, twelve apostles, three astrologers, one rooster — every hour, on the hour, since 1410.

We watched the noon show with three thousand other tourists, and then waited an hour and watched the one-o'clock show with the same three thousand, and then waited a third hour and watched the two-o'clock show with five hundred — because by then the heat had driven most people to ice cream stands. Avin demanded to see it a fourth time. We bargained him down to ice cream instead.

REFLECTIONS · CZECH REPUBLIC
Bridges, beer,
and 800-year-old
cobblestones.

Prague is what happens when a city is loved hard enough to survive. The Old Town Square is still where the merchants traded in 1300. The Charles Bridge is still where the saints stand in their original poses. Even the smell — of beer, and roast pork, and woodsmoke — feels like it has been the same for a thousand years.

We will remember the cobblestones under our shoes, the heaviness of the apartment's wooden door, the way the morning mist made the castle look like it was floating.

Favourite moment
Charles Bridge at 5:30am — empty, misty, ours alone.
Hardest moment
37°C in the Old Town Square at midday. The cobblestones reflected the heat upward.
If we come back…
Český Krumlov, Karlovy Vary, and a winter trip for the Christmas markets.
— Navin, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
