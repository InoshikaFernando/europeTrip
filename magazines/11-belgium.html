No. 11 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
chocolate country.

Belgium was a single day, and we spent it the way you spend a borrowed afternoon — quickly, gratefully, all of it. We drove down from Amsterdam with our cousin on the Friday morning, two hours along the A4/E19, and were parked in central Brussels by 09:30. Grand Place by 10:00. The Royal Palace by 11:30, the MIM by 12:30, the Atomium and Mini-Europe by 14:00, moules-frites somewhere in the middle — and back at our cousin's in Amsterdam by 20:00.

For one day it was ours: the headquarters of the European Union, the chocolate capital of the world — 220,000 tonnes a year, more than any other country — and home to a bronze boy, sixty centimetres tall, who has stood peeing into his fountain as the city's mascot since the 1300s.

The issue is short because the day was. But it was exactly what we needed: a fun day, a city small enough to hold in an afternoon, and a great deal of chocolate.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

COVER STORY · GRAND PLACE · DAY 23
A square covered
in gold.
By common consent one of the most beautiful squares in Europe — UNESCO-listed since 1998, and rebuilt in the 1690s from the rubble Louis XIV's artillery left behind.
Fri 31 Jul 2026Grand Place / Grote Markt90 min · square + Manneken Pis
📷 images/belgium/day-23-grand-place-aerial.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Grand Place at 10:00 — the Hôtel de Ville (left, with its 96-metre Gothic tower), the King's House (back), and the 17th-century guild houses (right) covered in gilded statuary.

You step in from a narrow lane and the city simply opens. Three sides are baroque guild houses — thrown up between 1696 and 1700 by the bakers, brewers, butchers, painters and weavers, each one taller and more thickly gilded than its neighbour. The fourth is the Hôtel de Ville, Gothic and severe from the 15th century, its tower climbing ninety-six metres into the grey. Every second summer the cobbles vanish under a carpet of a million flower petals; in odd years like ours they lie bare — stone worn to a shine by seven hundred years of markets and processions.

The capital of Europe — and the chocolate capital of the world.

A few streets on waits the Manneken Pis, sixty centimetres of bronze boy cheerfully piddling into his fountain as he has since at least 1388 (this cast dates to 1619). Almost every day, for reasons nobody can quite explain, he is dressed in a new costume — the City Museum keeps his wardrobe of a thousand and more. The morning we came he was a Belgian firefighter, which the children found tremendously funny. We did too.

CENTRAL BRUSSELS · DAY 23
The summer-only
Royal Palace.
Open to the public only between 22 July and 7 September each year, and free — with a mirror room whose ceiling is paved in 1.4 million beetle wings.
Fri 31 Jul 2026Place des Palais 745 min · throne room + Mirror Room
📷 images/belgium/day-23-royal-palace.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Royal Palace of Brussels — the King of the Belgians' official Brussels residence. Open to the public only in summer.

It is the King of the Belgians' seat in name — King Philippe and his family actually keep house at Laeken, out on the edge of the city — and the doors open to the rest of us only when he is away, every summer between 22 July and 7 September. You walk the throne room, the Empire Room, the Goya Room. But we stopped dead beneath the Mirror Room ceiling: Jan Fabre's Heaven of Delight, 2002, a chandelier and vault sheathed in 1.4 million iridescent green beetle wings.

1.4 million beetle wings on one Belgian ceiling. — Jan Fabre, 2002. Looks like jade.

From there to the MIM — the Musical Instruments Museum, behind the wrought-iron and glass of the 1899 Old England building. Fifteen hundred instruments, and an audioguide that wakes each one as you step near it: lean toward a 1750 harpsichord and a Scarlatti sonata unspools; drift to an Indonesian gamelan and the drums begin. The children LOVED it. Two floors, two hours, then lunch on the roof café with all of Brussels' rooftops below.

REFLECTIONS · BELGIUM
A gilded square,
a giant iron crystal,
a million beetle wings.

Belgium in a single day was a sprint — Grand Place, Manneken Pis, the Royal Palace, the MIM, the Atomium, Mini-Europe, moules-frites somewhere in the middle of it. By nightfall we were wrung out. We were also completely happy.

The Atomium and Mini-Europe were the children's day. Frites in a paper cone were ours. The chocolate belonged to everyone.

Favourite moment
Aviann counting the buildings at Mini-Europe — "The Eiffel Tower! Big Ben! That's the Atomium itself, here, in tiny!"
Hardest moment
The chocolate row at Sablon, one o'clock — where €500 would not have been enough, and we knew it.
If we come back…
Bruges. Ghent. Antwerp. Three Trappist monasteries. A Tomorrowland weekend.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
