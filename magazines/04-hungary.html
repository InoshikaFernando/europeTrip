No. 04 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
the Queen of
the Danube.

Until 1873, Budapest was two cities. Buda — old, royal, hilly, fortified — sat on the west bank of the Danube. Pest — flat, busy, trading — sprawled across the east. They could not have been more different. The Chain Bridge, opened in 1849, gave them their first permanent connection. Twenty-four years later, an act of parliament merged them, along with the smaller town of Óbuda, into a single capital.

We arrived from Vienna on a hot Thursday afternoon and went straight to the Széchenyi Baths — eighteen pools at temperatures from 18°C to 38°C, set inside a yellow neo-baroque palace, where old men played chess on floating boards.

This issue is about a city that learned to be one thing after spending centuries being two — and is still, somehow, both.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

Buda Castle has stood since the 13th century. From its terrace at sunset, the Danube glows orange, the Parliament catches fire across the river, and the Chain Bridge lights come on one by one.

Sunset from Fisherman's Bastion — the Hungarian Parliament across the Danube, golden in the last light.

The Castle District is where Budapest's royal story lives. King Béla IV began the first fortress here in the 1240s, after the Mongols sacked the lowland city of Pest. Successive kings rebuilt and expanded — adding a Renaissance court under Matthias Corvinus, a baroque palace under Maria Theresa, a neo-baroque rebuild after WWII destruction. Today the complex houses the Hungarian National Gallery, the Budapest History Museum, and the National Széchényi Library — but its most photographed feature is none of these. It is Fisherman's Bastion: a fairy-tale neo-Romanesque wall added in 1902, with seven towers (one for each Hungarian tribe that founded the country in 895 AD).

Queen of the Danube. — The title Budapest earned in the 19th century, as it transformed into a grand European capital.

We took the Sikló funicular up. Built in 1870, it is one of the oldest still in operation, and the kids loved the steep angle. From the top, we walked along the bastion wall as the sun lowered behind us and the Parliament across the Danube turned from white to gold to pink to a kind of fierce orange that lasted only fifteen seconds. Then the Chain Bridge lights came on. Then the Parliament lights came on. Then we cried a little, because the view was that good.

★ 18 Pools · 38°C

Széchenyi Baths

Built in 1913, the largest thermal complex in Europe. Yellow neo-baroque outside, geothermal water inside (38°C all year). Old men play chess at the edge of the outdoor pools, even in winter.

★ Built on Hot Springs

74°C from below

125 thermal springs supply Budapest. The Hungarian capital is the only major European city built directly on top of a geothermal field. The water at Széchenyi rises from 1,256 metres below the surface.

DUSK ON THE DANUBE · DAY 08
A river cruise
past the parliament.
The best way to see Budapest is from the Danube at dusk. The Parliament lights come on; the Chain Bridge glows; the city splits visually into Buda (up the hill) and Pest (along the river).
Thu 16 Jul 2026Vigadó tér to Margaret Island90 min · Legenda boat
📷 images/hungary/day-08-parliament-night.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Hungarian Parliament Building from the Danube — 691 rooms, 96 metres high, completed in 1904.

We boarded the cruise at 20:30. The boat slipped its mooring as the last sun touched the Citadel hill. We headed upstream, past the white stone of the Parliament — the largest building in Hungary, fifth-largest parliament in the world, designed in a Hungarian-Gothic style to mark Hungary's thousand-year anniversary in 1896. The lights along its façade flicker on one by one as you watch. Then the boat turns under the Margaret Bridge, slides past Margaret Island, and comes back down past the Chain Bridge — which by now is a string of light reflected on the black water.

Buda is the romance. Pest is the party. The river is the punctuation.

Avin fell asleep on my lap halfway through the cruise. The other two pointed quietly at every building, naming the ones they recognised from the morning. Aviann, who had been to Vienna two days earlier, said: "This is prettier than Schönbrunn." Avisha disagreed. We let them argue. The boat moved on.

REFLECTIONS · HUNGARY
A thermal bath,
a sunset bastion,
a bridge at night.

Budapest is a city you experience through your skin first, your eyes second, and your stomach third. The thermal baths are how you settle into the city. The Danube viewpoints are how you fall for it. And the goulash — well, the goulash is how you stay an extra day.

We came for one night. We left wishing we'd booked three. The Buda hills, the Pest cafés, the chess in the steaming pools — none of it had the rush of Vienna or the storybook density of Prague. It was slower. Older. Warmer. Better, in a way.

Favourite moment
Fisherman's Bastion at sunset. The Parliament caught fire across the river for fifteen seconds.
Hardest moment
Trying to get a sleeping Avin off the river-cruise boat at 22:30 without waking him.
If we come back…
A full week. Eger and the Bull's Blood wine region. Pécs in autumn.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
