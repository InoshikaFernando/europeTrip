No. 07 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
the country that
didn't fight.

Switzerland is small. You can drive its width in three hours. But its mountains are the steepest in Europe, its trains are the most punctual in the world, and its chocolate is the most consumed per capita on earth. We spent four nights here — two in alpine villages, two in cities — and saw what 700 years of strategic neutrality looks like up close.

In 1291 three small cantons signed a defensive pact in a meadow. That pact became the Swiss Confederation. Today there are 26 cantons, 4 official languages, and a country that has not fought a war since 1815.

This issue is about the Matterhorn (which we saw three times in three different light conditions), the Glacier Express (we did one leg), and a cheese demonstration in Moléson where a man in a felt hat made a 100kg wheel of Gruyère in front of us with a copper kettle.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

COVER STORY · GORNERGRAT · DAY 17
The Matterhorn
from above.
The Gornergrat railway has carried tourists up to 3,089 metres since 1898. From the top you see the Matterhorn, the Monte Rosa, and 29 other Alpine peaks above 4,000 metres.
Sat 25 Jul 2026Zermatt → Gornergrat6 hrs · train + walk + train back
The Matterhorn from the Gornergrat railway — 4,478 metres of perfectly triangular rock, in view almost the whole way up. First summited 14 July 1865.

Zermatt is car-free. You arrive at the train station and switch to electric taxis or your feet. The Gornergrat Railway departs from a building right next to the regular train station. It climbs 1,500 vertical metres in 33 minutes via cogwheel — the second-highest open-air railway in Europe. The Matterhorn comes into view almost immediately and stays there for the entire ride. By the time you reach the summit at 3,089 metres, you are looking at it from above.

Neutral since 1815. Wealthy since forever. — The most chocolate-eating country per capita on earth.

We had perfect weather. From the observation platform we could see 29 peaks above 4,000 metres, including the Monte Rosa (Switzerland's tallest), the Lyskamm, the Castor and Pollux twin summits, the Breithorn. We walked along the ridge for an hour. Avin found patches of summer snow and threw snowballs at his sisters in July.

MAISON CAILLER · BROC · DAY 17
A sweet
empire.
How a bitter bean from the Aztecs passed through the court of Emperor Charles V, was refined in the palaces of France, and finally became the milk chocolate the whole world knows — invented right here in Switzerland.
Sat 25 Jul 2026Maison Cailler · Brocself-guided tour + tasting
All five of us at Maison Cailler, Broc — founded 1819, the oldest Swiss chocolate brand still going, in its grand factory building (working since 1898).

Chocolate did not begin in Switzerland. It began with the Aztecs of Central America, who drank cacao bitter, thick and spiced. When Spanish conquistadors reached the New World in the 1500s the beans crossed the ocean and arrived at the royal court of Emperor Charles V — the Habsburg ruler of Spain, master of a realm so vast it was said the sun never set on it. His court sweetened the bitter drink with sugar and cane, and for almost a century kept it a guarded Spanish luxury.

Two emperors, one name: Charles V brought chocolate to Spain in the 1500s — not to be confused with Charles IV, who built the bridge we crossed in Prague 150 years earlier.

From Spain it spread to France through royal marriages — Spanish princesses who became French queens carried the habit to Paris, and by the reign of Louis XIV drinking chocolate was the fashion of Versailles. Yet it stayed a drink, a treat for aristocrats — until Switzerland changed everything. François-Louis Cailler opened his chocolate works in 1819. His son-in-law, Daniel Peter, blended in Nestlé's condensed milk in 1875 and invented milk chocolate. The bean that once belonged to emperors was, at last, for everyone — including five very happy tasters in Broc.

GENEVA + BERN · DAYS 18-19
Two cities,
two clocks.
Geneva: lakeside, French-speaking, home of the United Nations. Bern: medieval, German-speaking, home of the bears (literally — there's a bear pit). One day each. The contrast was perfect.
Sun 26 — Mon 27 Jul 2026Geneva → Bern2 days · old towns + lake jet + clock tower
📷 images/switzerland/day-18-geneva-jet-deau.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Jet d'Eau on Lake Geneva — 140 metres tall, 7,000 litres per second, visible from anywhere in the city.

Geneva is sometimes called the world capital of peace. The United Nations, the WHO, the Red Cross, the WTO — all headquartered here. We walked the lakeside promenade in the morning, climbed the old town for views, and stood close enough to the Jet d'Eau to feel the mist. Avin tried to count the seven thousand litres per second and gave up at twelve.

The Zytglogge has rung the hour for Bern since 1218. Albert Einstein used to walk past it on his way to the patent office.

Bern was the other half of the contrast: medieval, German-speaking, almost completely intact from the 13th century. Its UNESCO Old Town is surrounded on three sides by a sharp loop of the river Aare. The Zytglogge — the medieval clock tower — has shown the time, with a parade of animated figures, since 1218. Einstein lived in an apartment at Kramgasse 49 and developed special relativity here in 1905.

REFLECTIONS · SWITZERLAND
Mountains, milk,
and 700 years
of not picking sides.

Switzerland is what happens when geography (impossible mountains) meets policy (neutral since 1815). The result is a country that has saved its old towns, kept its languages, perfected its trains, and turned cheese into an export industry. The cities are tiny. The mountains are massive. The chocolate is everywhere.

We did this country in four days. We could not have done it in fewer. We could have done it in forty.

Favourite moment
The Matterhorn appearing through the train window at exactly the moment we crested the ridge.
Hardest moment
Bern at 32°C — apparently a heatwave by Swiss standards. The Aare river was 18°C, and we swam in it.
If we come back…
The Glacier Express end-to-end. Lauterbrunnen, Mürren, the Jungfraujoch.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
