No. 12 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
hygge.

We came in on a Sunday evening and left on the Monday afternoon — a single night stolen on the road to a sister waiting in Gothenburg. Between the two we walked ourselves ragged after the things you cannot skip: Nyhavn, the Little Mermaid, the Strøget, Tivoli Gardens, and every plate of smørrebrød we could reach.

The city is small, and it disarms you fast. Six hundred and sixty thousand people; 380 km of bicycle lane; 62% of them cycling, against Amsterdam's 38. It has been voted the world's happiest city more often than anywhere else, and within ten minutes you stop arguing: clean, calm, painted in colours that never shout, and strangers who smile at you on the bus.

A short issue, then. Twenty-two hours in Copenhagen. We made every one of them count.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

COVER STORY · NYHAVN · DAY 25
A canal of
painted houses.
Danish sailors and pirates dug their New Harbour here in the 1670s. Three centuries on, its colourful 17th-century townhouses, crowded restaurants and old wooden boats are the most photographed view in all of Denmark.
Sun 2 Aug 2026Nyhavn, Inner Harbour90 min · canal walk + dinner
📷 images/denmark/day-25-nyhavn-sunset.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
Nyhavn at 19:00 — the painted 17th-century townhouses turn gold in the low Danish sun. Hans Christian Andersen lived at no. 67.

For three centuries this was a working harbour — sailors off the Baltic, a canal dug by Swedish prisoners of war, Hans Christian Andersen writing in three of these very houses (numbers 18, 20 and 67) across his life. Then it fell hard: by the 1960s the cafés shut by eight and no one lingered. The 1980s repainted it, pedestrianised it, and handed it back as the postcard everyone now photographs. Touristy, yes; the restaurants cost a fortune, yes. But the colour is unreal, and the wooden boats along the quay have floated here a hundred years and more.

More bikes than cars, more parks than shopping malls, and the colour-coded waterfront of Nyhavn.

We took a canal-side table and let the evening happen: smørrebrød all round, a beer for me, a sparkling elderflower for Lana, sodas for the children, and a sky sliding from pale blue to peach to deep gold. Tour boats drifted past. Bicycle bells chimed. A jazz duo played at the end of the dock while the children ran the cobbles and lobbed imaginary pebbles into the water. It was, in a word, hygge.

★ Opened 1843

2nd oldest in the world

Only one amusement park on earth is older — Dyrehavsbakken, also in Copenhagen, dating to 1583. Tivoli opened in 1843, and in 1951 a visiting Walt Disney walked its lanes and went home to dream up Disneyland.

STRØGET + LITTLE MERMAID · DAY 26
The walking street
+ the smallest landmark.
One of Europe's longest pedestrian streets; one of the world's least impressive landmarks — a 1.25-metre bronze mermaid on a rock. We loved both.
Mon 3 Aug 2026Strøget → Langelinie3 hrs · walk + statue + train station
📷 images/denmark/day-26-stroget-morning.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
Strøget at 09:30 — 1.1km of pedestrian street, lined with cafés and Scandinavian design shops, the morning light slanting between the buildings.

She has sat on her rock since 1913 — a metre and a quarter of bronze Edvard Eriksen cast, they say, with his wife's face, after Andersen's tale. The city has never stopped fighting over her: beheaded twice, blown up once, painted red, painted pink, wrapped in a hijab, a niqab, hung twice with sex-toys, branded a "racist fish" by activists. Each time, Copenhagen mends her. Each time, the crowds return.

The world's most photographed disappointment. — and we mean that with affection.

Strøget, by contrast, simply delighted us: 1.1 km of car-free street strung with Royal Copenhagen porcelain, Bang & Olufsen, a Lego flagship where the children built a small Copenhagen of their own in twenty minutes. Then cinnamon buns, the city-hall square, and by noon the station — the X2000 south to Sweden, over the Øresund Bridge.

REFLECTIONS · DENMARK
Painted houses,
cinnamon buns,
a small bronze mermaid.

Copenhagen was the calmest stop of the whole trip — even the bicycles seemed to slow down for it. What stays is the colour of Nyhavn as the sun went low, the quiet smørrebrød lunches, and that word the Danes keep for cosy contentment and will not translate: hygge.

We'll come back in winter, we think, to walk Strøget under Christmas lanterns and see Tivoli lit up — they say it is one of the finest things in Europe at Christmas. We had it in summer. The children want it in lights.

Favourite moment
Nyhavn at golden hour. The painted houses, the boats, the jazz duo at the end of the dock.
Hardest moment
The Little Mermaid was disappointingly small. The kids laughed at her.
If we come back…
Christmas in Tivoli. Skagen in summer. Aarhus.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
