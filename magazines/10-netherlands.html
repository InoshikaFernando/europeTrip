No. 10 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
cousins.

The Netherlands was the family chapter. After three weeks of hotels, apartments, and short stays, we arrived in Amsterdam late on a Thursday night and were picked up at Schiphol by our cousin. We stayed in their home — a tall narrow Dutch house in a quiet neighbourhood — for three nights. We had three meals at their kitchen table. The kids played with their kids. We finally slept in.

We also saw a city that the people built themselves — 17% of Dutch land is reclaimed from the sea by centuries of dikes, windmills, and stubborn engineering. Amsterdam has more bicycles than residents. Anne Frank wrote her diary in a hidden annex four streets from where we ate stroopwafels.

This issue is shorter on monuments and longer on quiet afternoons. That is exactly what we needed.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

COVER STORY · ANNE FRANK HOUSE · DAY 24
A girl,
a diary,
a bookshelf.
For 25 months, eight people hid behind a bookshelf in an Amsterdam canal house. The youngest was thirteen. She kept a diary. After the war, her father survived and published it. The house is still there.
Sat 2 Aug 2026Prinsengracht 263-26790 min · pre-booked timed slot
📷 images/netherlands/day-23-anne-frank-house-exterior.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Anne Frank House on Prinsengracht. The bookshelf concealing the entrance to the Secret Annex is preserved exactly as it was on 4 August 1944.

The Anne Frank House is the most powerful museum we visited on the entire trip. It is not big. It is not loud. It does not need to be. You walk through the canal-side warehouse — Otto Frank's former business premises — and you climb the steep Dutch staircase to the bookshelf that for two years concealed the entrance to the Secret Annex. You bend through the hidden doorway. You enter four rooms where eight people hid. You see the pencil marks on the wall where Otto measured his daughters' growth. You see the magazine pictures Anne taped over her bed.

In spite of everything, I still believe that people are really good at heart. — Anne Frank, 15 July 1944.

We did not let Avin (4) in. He stayed downstairs with us in shifts. Aviann (8) understood enough. Avisha (11) understood almost all of it. When we walked out into the late afternoon sun by the Prinsengracht canal, none of us spoke for ten minutes. We bought ice cream at the canal-side stand and sat on a bench and watched the boats. The kids leaned against us. We held them tightly.

EUROSTAR ARRIVAL · DAY 22
Paris to Amsterdam
at 300 km/h.
Eurostar 9387 departed Paris Gare du Nord at 20:22. Arrived Schiphol Airport at 23:33. Coach 13, seats 72-76. Cousin pickup. Late dinner. Sleep.

📷 images/netherlands/day-22-eurostar-9387.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
Eurostar 9387 boarding at Gare du Nord at 19:52. The 20:22 departure carried us to Amsterdam in 3 hours 11 minutes.

The Eurostar to Amsterdam is a relatively new service — the direct route opened in 2018. It runs at up to 300 km/h on the dedicated high-speed line as far as the Belgian border, then settles to 250 km/h through the flat Dutch countryside. We boarded at Gare du Nord after a full Paris day, ate stale sandwiches we'd bought at a Carrefour near the station, and watched the sun set over the Champagne region from our reclining business-class seats.

God made the world, but the Dutch made the Netherlands. — 17% of the country is reclaimed from the sea.

At 23:33 we pulled into Schiphol. The cousin's SUV was waiting at the kerb. We piled in, four pieces of luggage and five tired bodies, and drove through the dark Dutch suburbs to a house we had never seen but had been invited to for three nights. The lights were on. The bedrooms were ready. We slept twelve hours.

REFLECTIONS · NETHERLANDS
A bookshelf,
a kitchen table,
a canal at dusk.

The Netherlands gave us the trip's most emotional museum visit and the trip's most ordinary family weekend. The contrast was perfect. We needed both. The Anne Frank House left a permanent mark on the children. The three nights at cousin's left a permanent mark on us — finally, finally, we slept in. The kids played with their cousins. We ate stroopwafels on the kitchen counter at 11pm.

Amsterdam is a slow city. The canals don't rush. The bikes don't rush. The cafés don't rush. It was exactly the pace we needed at this point in the trip.

Favourite moment
Stroopwafel fresh off the iron at Albert Cuyp. The syrup ran down our hands.
Hardest moment
Anne Frank exit. We didn't speak for ten minutes.
If we come back…
The Keukenhof tulip gardens in April. Utrecht. Maastricht. Maybe Friesland.
— Navin, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
