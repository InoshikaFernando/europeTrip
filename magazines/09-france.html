No. 09 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
two Frances.

We arrived in France twice, and each time it was a different country. First came Lourdes — a small town folded into the Pyrenees that swells every summer into one of the largest pilgrimage sites in the Catholic world. Then came Paris, which needs no introduction and gives none.

In Lourdes we walked with candles. Thousands of us, in the rain, around the Esplanade after dark, singing the same hymn in a dozen languages at once. Two nights later we were 276 metres above Paris at half past ten, the children pressed to the railing, naming the lights below them one by one.

A whispered prayer and a blazing city. Both, somehow, were France.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

A candle,
a song,
a procession.
We bought five small candles at a quarter to nine and joined a river of light already moving through the rain. By the time we understood what we were part of, the singing had begun.

HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Torch Procession on the Esplanade — thousands of candles, the Ave Maria sung in a dozen languages, every night from April to October at nine.

The rain was light and no one minded it. Within ten minutes we were among thousands, all walking the same slow circle of the Esplanade, all cupping the same small flame against the same soft rain. Then the Ave Maria began — in French, then Italian, then English, Polish, Spanish, Portuguese, Tagalog, and on into languages we could not name — the whole crowd handing the hymn from tongue to tongue without ever dropping it.

"I am the Immaculate Conception." — the words a fourteen-year-old named Bernadette said she heard at the grotto here, 25 March 1858. Six million pilgrims a year still come to listen.

Avin was four. He held his candle in both hands the whole way round and did not say a single word — not tired, not bored, just watching. Some places quieten a small child in a way you don't expect and can't engineer. This was one of them.

COVER STORY · EIFFEL TOWER · DAY 21
The tower
at midnight.
The trick to the Eiffel Tower is to refuse it by day and surrender to it at night. We watched the 22:00 sparkle from across the river, then rode the lift up into it. Five tickets, second floor, purchase #262010328650. Paris from 276 metres.
Wed 29 Jul 2026Champ de Mars + Trocadéro2 hrs · sparkle + lift up
📷 images/france/day-21-eiffel-sparkle-night.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Eiffel Tower's 22:00 sparkle show — 20,000 lights, 5 minutes, every hour after dark from 22:00 to 01:00.

By day the queues run to ninety minutes and the city below lies white and hazy; we skipped all of it. At ten the tower does something better than a view. From Trocadéro we watched twenty thousand lights race across its iron for five minutes flat — the whole sparkle — then crossed the river and rode the lift up into the thing itself. Cool air, thin crowd, and Paris laid out end to end: Sacré-Cœur to the north, Notre-Dame at the heart of it, the gold dome of Les Invalides burning directly below.

Paris is always a good idea. — Audrey Hepburn

Avin was four years old and wide awake at eleven at night, 276 metres above a city that would not stop glittering. The wind was cold even in July. The children leaned into the rail and called the lights like old friends — "That one's the Louvre!" "That one's Sacré-Cœur!" — and we let them stay up as long as they asked to, which was a long time. Five tickets, €65 in all: two adults at €23.50, three children at €6. Cheap, for a night none of us will let go of.

REFLECTIONS · FRANCE
A candle in Lourdes,
a lift up the Eiffel,
two ends of France.

Two Frances on the one map, and a four-hour train between them. The southern one prays by candlelight; the northern one blazes for the cameras. We rode from the first to the second in Coach 12, a baguette going hand to hand, and somehow both felt entirely true.

The tower at midnight is the one we'll be telling the children about when they're taller than us — how we lifted them to the rail and let them name the lights, how we came down at half past eleven and rode the Métro home with three of them asleep on our shoulders before the second stop.

Favourite moment
Avin's face at 22:50 on the second floor — four years old, all of Paris beneath him, saying nothing at all.
Hardest moment
The rain that never quite stopped in Lourdes. We walked back soaked to the skin — and would do it again tomorrow.
If we come back…
A week in Paris. The Loire châteaux. Brittany. Provence in lavender season.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
