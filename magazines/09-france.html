No. 09 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
two Frances.

France was a country of contrasts. The first was Lourdes — a small Pyrenean town that becomes, every summer, one of the world's largest Catholic pilgrimage sites. The second was Paris — the loudest, most photographed, most romantic city on earth.

In Lourdes we did the Torch Procession at the Sanctuary, where thousands of pilgrims carry candles around the Esplanade as the night settles. In Paris we climbed the Eiffel Tower at 22:30 on a Wednesday, with the sparkle show happening below us at 22:00 and the city stretching to every horizon.

Two cities. Two completely different experiences of France. Both unforgettable.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

COVER STORY · LOURDES · DAY 20
A candle,
a song,
a procession.
Every night from April to October, thousands of pilgrims march around the Sanctuary of Lourdes with candles and song. We joined them on a Tuesday night, all five of us, hands cupping flames in the rain.
Tue 28 Jul 2026Sanctuary of Our Lady of Lourdes90 min · 21:00 start
📷 images/france/day-20-lourdes-torch-procession.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Torch Procession on the Esplanade. Thousands of candles. Ave Maria sung in twelve languages. Every night, 21:00.

In 1858 a fourteen-year-old peasant girl named Bernadette Soubirous reported eighteen apparitions of the Virgin Mary in a small grotto by the Gave de Pau river. The Catholic Church investigated for four years and declared the visions authentic. Lourdes was transformed. Today the Sanctuary covers 51 hectares. Six million pilgrims come every year. They come for the Grotto, the spring water Bernadette dug up by hand, the underground Basilica of St Pius X (50,000 capacity), and the Torch Procession.

"I am the Immaculate Conception." — The lady's words to Bernadette, 25 March 1858.

We arrived at 20:45 and bought candles for the children. The procession started in light rain. Within ten minutes thousands of people were moving together around the Esplanade. The Ave Maria was sung in French, then Italian, then English, then Polish, then Spanish, then Portuguese, then Tagalog, then more languages we did not recognise. Avin held his candle in both hands the entire time. He did not say a single word.

COVER STORY · EIFFEL TOWER · DAY 21
The tower
at midnight.
22:00 sparkle show from Trocadéro. 22:30 summit by lift. 5 tickets, 2nd floor by lift, purchase #262010328650. Paris from 276 metres up.
Wed 29 Jul 2026Champ de Mars + Trocadéro2 hrs · sparkle + lift up
📷 images/france/day-21-eiffel-sparkle-night.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Eiffel Tower's 22:00 sparkle show — 20,000 lights, 5 minutes, every hour after dark from 22:00 to 01:00.

You should not climb the Eiffel Tower at noon. The queues are 90 minutes and the city below is white-hot and hazy. You should climb it at 22:30. That is what we did. We watched the 22:00 sparkle from across the river at Trocadéro — 20,000 lights flashing for five minutes — and then walked down to the base and rode the lift up. The city was cool. The crowd was thin. The view stretched from Sacré-Cœur in the north to Notre-Dame in the centre to the gold dome of Les Invalides directly below us.

Paris is always a good idea. — Audrey Hepburn

Avin was four years old, awake at 23:00, watching Paris from the second floor of the Eiffel Tower. We will hold this memory carefully. The tickets were €65 for all five (2 adults €23.50 + 3 children €6.00). The lift went up smoothly. The wind on the platform was cold even in July. The kids leaned over the rail and pointed at the lights — "That one's the Louvre!" "That one's Sacré-Cœur!" — and we let them stay up there as long as they wanted.

REFLECTIONS · FRANCE
A candle in Lourdes,
a lift up the Eiffel,
two ends of France.

France is two countries layered on the same map. The southern pilgrim France of Lourdes — quiet, devout, intimate. The northern capital France of Paris — loud, electric, monumental. We did the long route between them on a TGV, eating a baguette in Coach 12.

The Eiffel Tower at midnight is something we will tell the children about for the rest of their lives. We held them up to the railing. They pointed out the lights of the city. Then we rode the lift back down at 23:30 and Métro'd home, three sleepy children leaning against us.

Favourite moment
Avin's face on the Eiffel's second floor at 22:50. He could see the whole city below him.
Hardest moment
The Lourdes procession ended in rain. We walked back to the hotel soaking wet.
If we come back…
A week in Paris. The Loire châteaux. Brittany. Provence in lavender season.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
