No. 05 · A Letter from the Road
Welcome to
the Lake.

The Julian Alps rose out of the windscreen somewhere past the border, the road bent, and there it lay below us — glacier-blue water ringed by mountains, a single white church on a single small island. Slovenia was never on the plan, only a line on the long drive from Budapest to Venice. But some beauty will not let you pass it by. We meant to give it three hours. We gave it five.

The Slovenes love this place quietly. No postcards shouting from a rack, no theme-park signage — only a path around the water, a wooden dock, and a bell tower whose rope you may pull if you have a wish to make.

This is the shortest issue of our magazine — a single afternoon. And yet it marked us more deeply than whole countries have.

— The Munasinghes, 2026

COVER STORY · LAKE BLED · DAY 09
A pletna boat,
a small island,
a wish.
Bled Island is reached one way only: by pletna — a flat-bottomed wooden boat, worked by hand by oarsmen whose families have held the licences for centuries.
Fri 17 Jul 2026Lake Bled, Julian Alps90 min · boat + climb + bell
📷 images/slovenia/day-09-bled-island-church.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
The Church of the Assumption on Bled Island — built in the 17th century on a site of pre-Christian worship dating to ~700 BC.

Marko stood in the stern and leaned into two long oars, working them by hand — the standing stroke they say was invented here and rowed nowhere else in Europe. His great-great-grandfather had pulled the same water, he told us. The pletnas have crossed Lake Bled since 1590; there are only twenty-three of them, the licences handed down through families like his.

Ring the church bell once — and your wish may come true. The legend of Bled Island.

Ninety-nine stone steps climb from the dock to the church, and tradition says a groom must carry his bride up every one without resting. We watched a Slovenian couple attempt it in 30°C heat: the groom folded at step 87, and the bride walked the rest herself. Then we climbed too. Inside hangs a thick rope from the bell tower — pull it just so, make a wish, and the wish supposedly comes true. All three children pulled it. We will report back on whether the wishes came true.

AT THE LAKE'S EDGE · DAY 09
The cake that
changed everything.
Kremšnita — Bled's custard-and-cream cake — was invented at Hotel Park in 1953. We ordered one each, sat on the terrace, and watched the island while we ate.
Fri 17 Jul 2026Hotel Park, Bled1 hr · cake + lake-watching
📷 images/slovenia/day-09-kremsnita.jpg
HERO PHOTO · TBD
Kremšnita at Hotel Park, where it was invented in 1953. Custard, whipped cream, two squares of pastry, dusting of icing sugar.

The kremšnita is deceptively plain: two squares of crisp, paper-thin pastry, a thick layer of vanilla custard, a thicker one of whipped cream, a snowfall of icing sugar. That is all. Yet the pastry chef Ištvan Lukačević invented it right here at the Hotel Park in 1953, and the recipe has not moved since — more than 25,000 sold a year, the lake-facing terrace unchanged since the 1950s. From your table you see the lake, the island, the church, the cliff with the castle on top: exactly what generations of Yugoslav tourists saw before us, eating exactly the same cake, in 1953.

Two squares of pastry. One inch of custard. One inch of cream. Bled's only treaty.

There is something profound about ordering a cake that has been served from the same terrace for seventy-three years, looking at a view that has been beautiful for ten thousand. You feel small. You feel happy. You order a second one for the table.

REFLECTIONS · SLOVENIA
A single afternoon,
a thousand-year castle,
a wish on a bell.

Some chapters of a long trip pass in a blur. Slovenia was meant to be one of them — a stop on the way to Italy, nothing more. But Bled does something to you. The water is bluer than water should be. The mountains stand closer than the map admits. And the island bell rings one clear note that crosses the water and somehow finds you personally.

We came for three hours. We stayed five. Then we took the road to Italy, and every one of us looked back.

Favourite moment
Avin hauling the bell rope in both hands and shouting "I want a puppy!" — clear through the ringing bronze.
Hardest moment
Leaving. Not one of the children wanted to climb back into the car.
If we come back…
Stay overnight at Vila Bled. Hike to Velika Planina. Visit Lake Bohinj.
— Avinesh, Lana, Avisha, Aviann & Avin Munasinghe
